Overview

A rotary inverted pendulum that swings itself up from hanging down and then balances upright, on a custom embedded board. It is a classic control benchmark because it is nonlinear, underactuated, and unstable. One motor drives a free-swinging link, so lifting and balancing need two different controllers and a safe hand-over between them.

Linear control cannot handle the large swing-up motion. An energy-based controller first raises the pendulum's energy until it reaches the region where an LQR (linear quadratic regulator) can hold it upright. The project spans modeling, firmware and hardware. The model comes from the CAD design, the controllers run on an STM32, and micro-ROS streams live joint data to RViz for comparison with theory.

Process

  1. Derive the model

    Modified DH parameters, a \(6 \times 2\) Jacobian and Lagrangian dynamics are derived in MATLAB, with mass properties from the CAD design.

  2. Kick and swing up with energy

    The arm swings the pendulum up in two stages.

    • A short kick of half a second starts the motion.
    • An energy law, \(U = k_E\,\Delta E\,\dot\alpha\cos\alpha\), then pumps energy into the pendulum until it nears the top.

  3. Balance with LQR

    The LQR gain comes from the discrete-time Riccati equation, with the weights chosen by Bryson's rule.

    • When \(|\alpha-\pi|\lt 0.25\) rad, control switches from swing-up to LQR.
    • If \(|\alpha-\pi|\gt 0.5\) rad, control returns to swing-up.

  4. Estimate the state

    A FIR filter and a Kalman filter estimate joint velocities from the two encoders.

  5. Stream and compare

    micro-ROS streams the joint states to RViz, where measured motion is compared with the motion computed from the model and the Jacobian.

Results

The pendulum swings up and balances on the real hardware. Measured and theoretical joint positions agree, and the velocity error between the model and the hardware stays below 0.01 rad/s. The stair-step look of the theory curves comes from the 55 to 57 Hz micro-ROS link, not from the model.
